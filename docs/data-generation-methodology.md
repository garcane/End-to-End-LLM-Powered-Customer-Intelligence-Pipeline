# Data generation methodology

**Synthetic Professional Membership Engagement and Retention Dataset**

This document is the deliverable that matters. Anyone can upload synthetic CSV files;
what makes them defensible is a written account of the data-generating process, the
business rules encoded in it, and the checks that confirm the result behaves the way it
was supposed to.

---

## 1. Why synthetic, and why hybrid

The project needs a member dimension with churn outcomes **and** event attendance,
website activity, login history, learning participation, marketing engagement,
longitudinal behaviour and free-text feedback. No public dataset has all of that.

The approach follows the recommendation in `Data Recomendations.md`:

```text
IBM Telco churn structure (calibration)
        +
synthetically generated membership activity
        =
complete membership intelligence dataset
```

The seed is the **extended IBM Cognos Telco Customer Churn sample** -- six workbooks
covering 7,043 customers, held in `IBM Source Files/`. That is materially richer than the
flat Kaggle CSV: it adds exact age, satisfaction scores, a two-level churn-reason
taxonomy, referral counts, offers, lifetime value and per-service detail.

IBM contributes **structure, marginals and evidence**, not rows. No IBM record is copied
into the output. The full measurement is in
[`docs/ibm-telco-calibration.md`](ibm-telco-calibration.md), regenerated by
`python -m src.generators.calibrate`.

### What is carried over

| Measured from IBM | Value | Used for |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Tenure distribution (deciles) | mean 32.4 months | Bootstrapped to shape `join_date` |
| Exact age | mean 46.5 | The `age_band` distribution, re-weighted for a professional body |
| Month-to-month contract share | 51.3% | Base rate for `auto_renewal = False` |
| Automatic payment share | 43.5% | Cross-check on the same (from the *original* workbook) |
| Paperless billing share | 59.2% | Base rate for `communication_consent` |
| Referrer share / mean referrals | 45.8% / 4.27 | The whole `member_referrals` table |
| Satisfaction distribution | 1-5 marginal | Target for `member_feedback.rating` |
| Churn category taxonomy | 5 categories | Structure of `churn_category` -> `cancellation_reason` |
| Churn rate | 26.5% | Sense-check on cumulative lapse rate |
| Charge ratio, churned/retained | 1.215 | Evidence for the fee-sensitivity term |

### What is deliberately dropped

City, zip code, latitude, longitude and zip-level population are US-specific and
meaningless for a UK-centred membership body. Gender, marital status and dependants are
sensitive attributes that add nothing to an engagement or retention model, and the
recommendation explicitly advises against generating unnecessary personal fields. The
telco service columns -- phone service, multiple lines, streaming TV -- have no
membership analogue; copying them across is exactly what makes a portfolio project look
like a re-skinned churn tutorial.

### Two columns in the seed are leaked

This matters enough to state plainly. The extended sample contains two columns that were
assigned **after** the churn outcome was known:

| Column | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| `Satisfaction Score` | Scores of 1 and 2 churn at exactly 100%; scores of 4 and 5 at exactly 0%. Only score 3 is mixed. |
| `Churn Score` | IBM's own model output. Retained customers top out at 80, churned customers start at 65: the classes are nearly separable. |

Either one would produce a model with near-perfect accuracy and no real-world value.
`src/generators/ibm_seed.py` detects both automatically -- it flags any predictor with
value groups whose churn rate is exactly 0 or exactly 1 across a meaningful number of rows
-- and excludes them from the analogue fit. Their marginal distributions are still useful
and are retained; only the conditional relationship with churn is discarded.

### Three coefficients are estimated, not asserted

The recommendation's formula specifies nine coefficients. The four extension terms were
originally asserted from domain judgement. Three of them are now **fitted from the seed**.

A logistic model is fitted on mapped analogue predictors, on the comparable
sub-population: customers who have internet service (n = 5,517, churn rate 31.8%,
in-sample AUC 0.789). Phone-only customers are excluded, and that exclusion is not
cosmetic -- including them **inverts the sign** on service breadth, because a cheap
single-product customer is not the same thing as a disengaged one. Restricted properly,
churn falls monotonically from 46.9% at one service to 4.9% at eight.

| IBM predictor | Fitted | Membership feature | Coefficient used |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| `high_charge` | +0.765 | `fee_increase` | **+0.77** (was +0.30 asserted) |
| `multi_service` | -0.249 | `multi_service_user` | **-0.25** (was -0.45 asserted) |
| `referred` | -0.206 | `no_recent_advocacy` | **+0.21** (sign inverted; new term) |
| `auto_payment` | -0.532 | `auto_renewal` | -0.50, from the recommendation -- corroborated |
| `long_tenure` | -0.638 | `long_tenure` | -0.40, from the recommendation -- corroborated |
| `month_to_month` | +1.948 | _none_ | Annual terms have no contract-length analogue |

### A trap in the extended workbook

The extended sample relabels the original four payment methods into three, and the
relabelling is **lossy**. Every manual `Electronic check` customer -- a third of the
sample -- is folded into `Bank Withdrawal` alongside genuine automatic payers:

```text
original                         ->  extended
Bank transfer (automatic)  21.9%     Bank Withdrawal  55.5%
Electronic check           33.6%   /
Credit card (automatic)    21.6%     Credit Card      39.0%
Mailed check               22.9%   /  Mailed Check     5.5%
```

Taken at face value, the extended labels put the automatic-payment share at **95.6%**
rather than **43.5%**, and mis-specify the predictor that maps onto `auto_renewal`. The
calibration therefore reads payment method from the original workbook, where the
`(automatic)` marker survives. Correcting it moved the fitted coefficient from -0.43 to
**-0.53** -- a near-exact match for the -0.50 published in `Data Recomendations.md`,
where before it had looked like a loose one.

The fourth extension, `low_campaign_engagement`, was **reduced from +0.35 to +0.25**:
IBM's closest analogue (offer take-up) returned +0.03, i.e. no effect at all. It is kept
on domain grounds -- unopened member communications are a real warning sign -- but
weighted down, because the seed does not support the original figure. The fifth,
`student_to_full_transition`, has no analogue in the seed and remains a stated domain
judgement.

### The seed added a table

IBM records `Referred a Friend` and `Number of Referrals`, and referral behaviour turns
out to be one of the strongest leak-free signals in the whole sample: retained customers
referred **4.8x** as many people as churned ones. Nothing in the original table list
captured advocacy at all.

So the generator now produces `member_referrals`, a member-to-member graph. IBM records
only the count, never who referred whom; joining the edge here -- linking members acquired
by referral back to an earlier, more advocacy-prone member -- turns a column into a
network. Advocacy is modelled as zero-inflated, because the seed is: 54.2% of customers
referred nobody, while those who did referred 4.27 on average. A single rate parameter
cannot reproduce both facts.

### The seed is censored

IBM observes tenure only up to **72 months**, because the sample covers a fixed window.
The true tenure tail is unobservable. The generator bootstraps the empirical distribution,
stretches it by 1.6x (annual membership terms are not month-to-month telco contracts), and
extrapolates an exponential tail beyond the censoring point for the longest-standing 5% of
members. That extrapolation is an assumption, not a measurement, and is flagged as such in
the calibration report.

### Running without the source files

The workbooks are not committed. If they are absent, the generator falls back to the
published marginals of the same dataset and records
`"source": "published-marginals"` in the manifest, so any extract can be traced to which
path produced it. Place them in `IBM Source Files/` and run
`python -m src.generators.calibrate` to measure directly.

## 2. Structure of the generator

```text
src/generators/
├── config.py       scale profiles, reference vocabularies, model coefficients
├── ibm_seed.py     IBM Telco calibration: measurement, leakage audit, analogue fit
├── calibrate.py    CLI that writes the calibration JSON and the calibration report
├── utils.py        deterministic RNG streams, streaming CSV writers, date maths
├── members.py      member dimension + latent behavioural traits
├── catalogues.py   events, campaigns, courses
├── features.py     feature extraction + the churn data-generating process
├── templates.py    free-text feedback and placeholder insight narrative
├── activity.py     per-member simulation, churn assignment, row emission
├── data_quality.py data-quality check results
└── run.py          CLI orchestrator
src/validation/
└── validate.py     structural, volume, signal and seed-fidelity validation
```

Run it with:

```bash
python -m src.generators.calibrate             # measure the IBM seed (once)
python -m src.generators.run --scale full      # 25,000 members, ~6 minutes
python -m src.validation.validate
```

The calibration is cached to `data/seed/ibm_calibration.json`, so generation never needs
Excel or the source workbooks.

Scale profiles are `dev` (1,000 members, ~25s), `demo` (8,000) and `full` (25,000).
Members are processed in chunks and rows are streamed straight to CSV, so peak memory
stays in the tens of megabytes even at full scale — it runs comfortably on a laptop
with 8 GB of RAM.

Every run is reproducible from `--seed`, and each table draws from its own named RNG
sub-stream, so changing the number of random draws in one generator does not shift the
output of all the others.

## 3. The member as a latent person

Each member is created with traits that are never exported but drive everything
observable:

* `engagement` — a Beta(2.2, 2.8) draw, multiplied by a membership-grade uplift, shifted
  by acquisition-channel quality, and nudged upwards by tenure (members who have already
  survived several renewals are, by selection, more engaged).
* `event_affinity`, `learning_affinity`, `email_affinity`, `web_affinity` — engagement
  plus independent noise, so channel preferences differ between members.
* `satisfaction` — drives feedback ratings and unsubscribe rates. Ratings are mapped
  onto the IBM satisfaction distribution through a quantile transform, centred on the
  response-weighted population, so the marginal of the ratings actually present in
  `member_feedback` matches the seed rather than whatever a linear rescale happens to
  produce. Getting this wrong is easy and quiet: feeding a non-uniform latent score
  straight into a quantile map produces a distribution nothing like the target.
* `advocacy` — zero-inflated, driving `member_referrals`. Calibrated so that both the
  share of members who refer anyone and the mean among those who do track the seed.
* `drift` — a year-on-year multiplicative trend, `Normal(-0.04, 0.30)`. This is the term
  that produces genuinely *declining* members rather than members who are uniformly
  quiet.
* `price_sensitivity` — interacts with fee increases at renewal.

Demographics are sampled as a chain, not independently:
`age_band → career_stage → membership_type`. A 22-year-old is very unlikely to be a
Fellow, and the dataset should reflect that.

## 4. Behaviour over time

For each active week the member's activity level is:

```text
level(w) = exp(drift × years_since_join) × seasonality(w)
```

`seasonality` encodes a spring and autumn programme, an August lull and a near-total
shutdown over Christmas. Events are scheduled on the same seasonal curve and are
weekday-weighted, so event attendance and web traffic move together for the right
reason rather than by coincidence.

Activity volumes per week are then Poisson draws:

| Table | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Logins | `1.05 × web_affinity × level` per week |
| Extra web sessions | `0.30 × web_affinity × level` per week |
| Event registrations | `7.5 × event_affinity × level` per year |
| Learning activities | `3.5 × learning_affinity × level` per year |
| Referral invitations | `1.92 × advocacy × level` per year, advocates only |

Weeks with no web sessions are not written at all, which is both realistic and what
keeps `website_activity` near 1.6 million rows rather than 4 million.

Marketing sends are driven by the campaign catalogue and its `target_segment`: a member
receives a campaign only if they have consented, match the segment, and have not
previously unsubscribed. Open, click, convert and unsubscribe are a strict nested
hierarchy, so `clicked` can never be true where `opened` is false.

## 5. The churn data-generating process

This is the part that determines whether the dataset is worth anything.

At **each renewal anniversary**, features are measured from behaviour that has already
been generated, and only from behaviour that precedes the renewal date:

* 90-day and prior-90-day login counts (giving both a level and a trend)
* event attendance and learning completions over the last 365 days
* campaign open rate over the last 180 days
* worst feedback rating over the last 365 days
* whether an event was attended within 90 days of joining
* tenure, auto-renewal status, and whether the renewal payment failed
* whether this renewal carries a large fee increase for a price-sensitive member
* whether the member is moving off the student rate
* whether they have referred anyone in the last year

Those features go into the logistic model published in `Data Recomendations.md`:

```python
churn_log_odds = (
    -2.2
    + 0.9 * inactive_90_days
    + 0.7 * negative_feedback
    + 0.8 * failed_payment
    + 0.6 * no_event_attendance
    + 0.5 * declining_logins
    - 0.7 * completed_learning
    - 0.6 * attended_first_90_days
    - 0.5 * auto_renewal
    - 0.4 * long_tenure
)
```

with five extensions. Three are **fitted from the IBM seed** (section 1); two are stated
domain judgements:

```python
    + 0.77 * fee_increase                 # IBM: high_charge,    +0.765
    - 0.25 * multi_service_user           # IBM: multi_service,  -0.249
    + 0.21 * no_recent_advocacy           # IBM: referred,       -0.206 (sign inverted)
    + 0.25 * low_campaign_engagement      # domain, reduced: IBM analogue showed no effect
    + 0.45 * student_to_full_transition   # domain: no IBM analogue exists
```

The outcome is then sampled:

```python
churn_probability = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-churn_log_odds))
churn_flag = np.random.binomial(1, churn_probability)
```

**The intercept was not tuned.** With the published −2.2, the realised lapse rate comes
out at roughly 8-9% per renewal decision and ~20% cumulative over the five-year window,
which is a plausible figure for a professional body. No calibration offset is applied.

Once a renewal lapses, **every activity row dated on or after that date is discarded**,
across all seven activity tables, and the member is excluded from later scoring runs.
The only exception is the cancellation survey, which by definition arrives at the exit.
This ordering — behaviour, then features, then outcome, then truncation — is what makes
the label learnable rather than circular.

## 6. Predictions and insights

`model_predictions` simulates six monthly scoring runs. The "model" sees the true
log-odds plus `Normal(0, 0.85)` noise, which yields out-of-time AUC around 0.77: good
enough to be useful, far enough from 1.0 to be honest. Risk bands are set at 0.10 and
0.25 rather than at 0.35/0.65, because with a 9% base rate fixed mid-range bands would
put 95% of members in the lowest band and leave the churn-risk report with nothing to
display.

`top_risk_factor_1..3` are the three largest positive contributions to that member's
log-odds, so the explanation shown in Power BI is arithmetically consistent with the
score next to it.

`ai_insights` rows are **template-assembled placeholders**, flagged as
`validation_status = 'Template'`. They let the semantic model and the AI Insight Centre
page be built before the Ollama/Gemini stage exists; the live pipeline overwrites them,
keyed on `prediction_run_id` so a narrative can never drift onto a stale score.

## 7. Validation

`python -m src.validation.validate` runs 84 checks in six groups.

**Structure** — primary keys unique across all twelve keyed tables; every foreign key
resolves; every categorical value inside its declared domain; nested booleans consistent
(`clicked → opened → delivered`); `assessment_score` present if and only if a course was
completed.

**Causal consistency** — no login, web, event, learning, marketing, feedback or
prediction row dated on or after a member's `churn_date` (cancellation surveys excepted);
`churn_date > join_date`; churned members have no forward renewal date and active
members all have one.

**Volume** — row counts per member against the ranges recommended in
`Data Recomendations.md`. Enforced on the `full` profile; reported as warnings on the
smaller profiles, which deliberately cut the campaign catalogue and the number of
scoring runs.

**Signal** — the important group. A logistic regression is fitted from scratch (scipy
L-BFGS, no sklearn dependency) on `member_training_features`, holding out the most
recent third of observations as an out-of-time test set. The suite asserts that:

* the realised lapse rate is between 5% and 22% per renewal decision;
* the ceiling AUC from the true propensity is between 0.72 and 0.95;
* a refitted model reaches out-of-time AUC above 0.68;
* every fitted coefficient has the same sign as the one that generated the data;
* fitted and generating coefficients correlate at r ≥ 0.85.

**Business plausibility** — email open rate, click-through rate, event attendance rate,
negative-feedback share, and a direct check that members who lapse logged in less often
in the 90 days before renewal than members who renewed.

**IBM seed fidelity** — the check that closes the loop. The parts of the dataset taken
from the seed are compared back against it: the share of members who refer anyone and the
mean among those who do, the age-band distribution, the survey rating distribution against
IBM satisfaction, and confirmation that the three IBM-estimated coefficients still match
the calibration file rather than having drifted.

A representative full-scale run:

```text
annual lapse rate                  8.4% per renewal decision
ceiling AUC (true propensity)      0.772
out-of-time AUC of a refitted model 0.772
fitted vs true coefficients        r = 0.965
email open rate                    42.0%
event attendance rate              64.6% of registrations
lapsing vs renewing members        4.5 vs 8.9 logins in the prior 90 days
```

## 8. Privacy

No names, addresses, dates of birth, email addresses, telephone numbers or payment
details are generated. Age is banded. Every member, event, course and campaign is
fictional. There is nothing in this dataset to de-identify, because there was never an
identity in it.

## 9. Known simplifications

Stated plainly, because a generator that claims to be realistic in every respect is not
credible:

* Churn is evaluated **only at renewal anniversaries**. Mid-term cancellation happens in
  reality; here it is folded into the renewal decision.
* Members never rejoin after lapsing. There is no win-back readmission path, even though
  `Win-back` campaigns exist in the catalogue.
* The advocacy effect is weaker than the seed's. IBM's churned customers referred 0.21x
  as many people as retained ones; here the ratio is around 0.6. The two are not directly
  comparable -- IBM counts lifetime referrals at a single cross-section, and its referral
  counts correlate with protective factors this model treats as independent -- but the
  gap is real and is reported as an advisory check rather than papered over.
* Survey response is self-selected: a member must have opened a survey campaign to
  answer one, so respondents skew satisfied. The rating quantile map is therefore centred
  on the response-weighted population rather than the whole membership, which is what
  makes the *observed* ratings match the seed. Response bias is still modelled; it is the
  calibration that accounts for it.
* `predicted_lifetime_value` is a revenue figure, whereas IBM's `CLTV` is a bounded index.
  Only the concept transfers; the scale and spread do not, and no attempt is made to match
  them.
* Tenure beyond 72 months is extrapolated, not evidenced (section 1).
* Event capacity is not enforced. `Waitlisted` is sampled at a flat 3% rather than
  emerging from oversubscription.
* Renewals that pre-date the observation window are assumed successful — the member is
  still present, so by construction they must have renewed. This creates a mild survivor
  effect in the earliest cohorts.
* `website_activity` is weekly, so within-week sequencing is lost. This was a deliberate
  trade for a table of 1.6 million rows instead of 4 million.
* Free-text feedback is drawn from a sentence bank rather than a language model. It is
  varied enough to exercise summarisation and classification, but it is not open-ended
  natural language.
* Region is a single flat attribute; there is no organisation hierarchy or household
  structure.
